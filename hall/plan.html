S-01

Is the office wall load-bearing?

Almost certainly not. Spend an hour proving it before the first board comes off.

Likely verdict: not load-bearing

Plasterboard on metal studs (a cloison de distribution) is not designed to carry anything. In this house the structure is the 50 cm stone outer walls and the party wall, and the floors span between them. The notary's description lists a cellier rather than an office, which suggests the office walls went in later. Old houses still hide surprises, though: a timber post inside a lining, or a beam end resting on a partition. The checks below rule those out.

Eight checks, about an hour

  1. Knock along the wall.Hollow everywhere, with solid spots every 40–60 cm (the studs), means plasterboard on a frame. A dull, solid sound everywhere means masonry: stop and look further.
  2. Measure the thickness at the door frame.About 72 mm (13 + 48 + 13) or 98 mm means a standard partition. 15 cm or more could be brick or block.
  3. Find the studs.A magnet finds the screws, which sit about every 30 cm vertically. A stud finder also works.
  4. Look inside.Switch the circuit off, unscrew a socket or switch and shine a torch into the box hole. You should see metal C-studs and an empty cavity. A 10 × 10 cm hole at 1 m height is fine too, since the wall is coming out anyway.
  5. Check the top.Is there a beam (poutre) running across above the wall? Does the ceiling run continuously over it? A boxed-in beam shows as a bulkhead.
  6. Look at what is directly above on the 1st floor.The kitchen (22.6 m²) and living room (20 m²) are large open rooms. If no wall stands on this line upstairs and the floor doesn't sag there, the partition carries no floor load.
  7. Check the joist direction.The garage ceiling often shows the floor structure. Joists running parallel to the partition can't rest on it. Joists running across it still won't rest on a plasterboard wall, but look for a post.
  8. Remove boards from one side first.Take one face off, look at the bare frame, then take the second face off. Remove the studs last.
Stop and call a structural engineer if you see
  • a timber or steel post, or a doubled or boxed stud, inside the frame
  • a beam end, lintel or trimmer (chevêtre) resting on the wall
  • brick, block or carreau de plâtre instead of a hollow frame
  • new cracks upstairs, sticking doors or a ceiling that drops as you work

A bureau d'études structure or an architect charges about 300–600 € for a site visit and written note. Prop the ceiling with acrow props (étais, about 10 € per day to hire) while you wait.

The party wall is a separate matter

The right-hand 50 cm stone wall on the plan may be the wall you share with the neighbours (mur mitoyen). You can line it and screw furniture to it. Cutting chases or recesses into it needs their agreement (Code civil, art. 662).

Asbestos and lead

The 2024 reports found no asbestos in the listed materials and no lead in the wall and ceiling paint (every reading was 0). A modern plasterboard partition can't contain asbestos anyway. Wear an FFP3 mask all the same, because gypsum dust irritates the lungs.

Electrics: what goes, what's new

Electrical plan for Layout A, using French plan symbols. Every socket and switch sits at 1.10 m, with cables dropping from the ceiling.

Taking out the old devices

  1. Switch off the breaker(s), then test every conductor with a VAT voltage tester. A screwdriver tester isn't proof.
  2. Photograph each box before you disconnect it. A partition can carry a cable on to another room, such as the buanderie, so trace where each one goes.
  3. Cap each live-side cable with a Wago connector inside an accessible junction box, or pull it back to the ceiling. Never bury a junction in a wall or leave it at floor level.
  4. Look for anything else in the wall: a radiator pipe, a thermostat cable, the doorbell, TV coax or RJ45.

The new hall

  • Lights: two ceiling points on DCL boxes, one in the old corridor and one reusing the office point. Wire them on two-way switches or push buttons with a télérupteur.
  • Switches: one by the front door on the latch side, and one by the buanderie door. Both at 1.10 m.
  • Sockets: two 2P+T 16 A sockets at 1.10 m, one by the wardrobe for the vacuum and one by the bench for charging.
  • Cable sizes: 1.5 mm² on a 16 A breaker for lighting and 2.5 mm² on a 20 A breaker for sockets, in ICTA conduit inside the board. The electrician matches them to your existing circuits.
  • Panel: put every ground-floor circuit behind its own 30 mA RCD (interrupteur différentiel 40 A) and label it "RDC". In a flood you switch off the ground floor and keep power upstairs.
What the rules say

Under NF C 15-100, socket centres sit between 5 cm and 1.30 m above the finished floor. The 1.30 m limit comes from the accessibility rules for new homes, and 1.10 m fits inside it. DIY electrical work on your own home is legal, but it must meet NF C 15-100, and your insurer can refuse a fire claim caused by faulty work. A partial modification like this doesn't need a Consuel certificate; that applies to new or fully rewired installations.

G-04

Permits and rules in France

The hall itself needs no permit. The flood measures and the insurance paperwork are where the rules come in.

No planning permission for the hall

Removing a non-load-bearing interior partition in a house doesn't change the façade, the use or the floor area. It needs no déclaration préalable and no permis de construire. If a professional takes out the partition, they may ask for a repérage amiante avant travaux (pre-works asbestos survey) because the house predates 1997. Show them the 2024 report; a targeted survey costs about 150–300 € if they insist.

Façade work: check first

Anything fixed permanently to the outside, such as barrier frames screwed to the façade or a new window, may need a déclaration préalable. Near a listed monument, the Architecte des Bâtiments de France must also approve it. Ask the Saint-Julien service urbanisme before drilling the façade. Fully removable barriers need nothing.

Flood-risk plan (PPRN)

Your November 2024 risk statement says the approved PPRN (15 June 2020) doesn't place the house in an exposed zone. A revision was prescribed on 6 Sep 2023 and covers the whole commune until its study area is set. It imposes no works yet, but new rules may follow. Recent disasters: floods and mudslides on 6 Sep 2008 and 9 Jun 2024. Check the commune's page on Géorisques or ask the DDT 74 once a year.

Fonds Barnier aid

The FPRNM ("Fonds Barnier") can pay up to 80% of flood-proofing works for homeowners, such as barriers or raised equipment. The works must be required by an approved PPR, or identified by a vulnerability survey in an area with a PAPI (flood prevention programme). Your house isn't in an exposed zone of the approved plan, so eligibility depends on a PAPI and a survey. Ask the DDT 74 or the mairie before you buy a barrier, because aid usually can't be granted after the purchase.

Insurance (auto-réparation)
  • You're free to do the work yourself with the 1,800 €. Keep every receipt and the before-and-after photos. If you claim again later, the insurer can reduce the payout for damage that was never repaired.
  • Keep Bouchet BTP's invoice or report. It proves the cause was fixed, which matters for future claims and for a future sale, when you'll disclose this claim.
  • Water claim: report it within 5 working days. CatNat claim: within 30 days of the decree's publication.
Electrics

NF C 15-100 applies to the new work. DIY is legal; panel work by an electrician is the safe choice. A partial change needs no Consuel. Keep the 2024 electrical report with your papers.

Party wall

Lining or fixing to a mur mitoyen is fine. Chases, recesses or anything built into it need the neighbours' agreement (Code civil, art. 662).

Waste, noise, safety
  • Plasterboard goes in the plâtre skip at a Communauté de Communes du Genevois déchetterie, free with a residents' badge. Never put it in household bins.
  • Noisy DIY hours are set by Haute-Savoie prefectoral order and the mairie: normal daytime on weekdays, shorter hours on Saturdays, very limited on Sundays. Check before a Sunday session and warn the neighbours.
  • Every home must have at least one smoke alarm (NF EN 14604). Refit it if it comes down during the work.
  • The gas boiler must be serviced every year.
